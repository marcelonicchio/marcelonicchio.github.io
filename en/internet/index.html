Pre-web context. Part of this history is autobiographical because little household documentation from the BBS years survived. Where contemporary material exists — such as Mirantte News and photographs from the PSINet period — it is kept distinct from memory-based narrative.

1992–1996 — BBS culture

Nicchio was an intensive user of BBS services including SP Online/STI and Mandic. This period marked his first sustained contact with digital communities, menu-driven navigation, file exchange, messaging and network culture before the commercial web became mainstream in Brazil.

In parallel, he created Minduim BBS, a personal experiment built with RemoteAccess software and two telephone lines. The project was experimental and non-commercial. No screenshots, photographs or known backups survive; the visual below is explicitly presented as an AI reconstruction based on factual elements and period memory, never as a historical capture.

1996 — transition to the commercial internet

From 1996 onward, Nicchio began using commercial internet access through STI. This phase overlapped with his music and cultural work and helps show that artistic creation and networked technology coexisted within the same trajectory.

No public documentation has yet been recovered that details this initial internet-use period. The site therefore keeps it as autobiographical information and does not inflate it into unsupported claims of pioneering status.

2000 — PSINet and corporate hosting

In 2000, Nicchio worked at PSINet, an international internet infrastructure and hosting company, in commercial work related to Corporate Hosting.

A small number of photographs with the PSINet team survive in the personal archive. They may serve as contextual visual documentation but will not be presented as proof of job title, scope or results beyond what the images themselves support.

2004–2008 — Mirantte News

Mirantte News was a larger-scale digital venture in this phase. Nicchio describes the project as his creation and operation, with Luiz Kechichian as the investing partner. The portal brought together roughly twenty service categories and combined editorial content, business pages and location tools.

Two contemporary artifacts survive: a capture of the homepage and an article about the project. The article identifies Marcelo Nicchio as director of the site, describes the commercial hot-sites, the categories and the integration with MapLink for maps and routing.

The surviving material directly supports the portal's existence, positioning and Nicchio's public role as director. Founder and ownership language remains attributed to the autobiographical account until suitable minimal corporate-registry evidence is added.

Continuity: digital culture before Search

One function of this page is to prevent the digital trajectory from being reduced to “digital marketing.” BBS culture, commercial internet, hosting, editorial writing about technology and portal development all appear before the specialization in SEM. Search came later as a professional layer on top of an already long relationship with digital systems.
