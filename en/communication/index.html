Early writing in a digital environment

In 1993, Nicchio maintained a cultural column on Tremonte BBS. The material did not survive: this was a pre-commercial-web environment, from a period when screenshots and home archiving were uncommon. The episode therefore remains autobiographical, while fitting the documented timeline of his active use of BBS networks at the time.

Its relevance is less about claiming “pioneer” status than about context: writing, cultural curation and digital environments were already intertwined before internet access became mainstream in Brazil.

Metrópole — Rádio Nova Paulista FM

Between 1994 and 1995, Marcelo Nicchio hosted and curated the Metrópole radio program, broadcast Monday through Friday from 8 p.m. to 10 p.m. on Rádio Nova Paulista FM 103.7 in São Paulo. According to his biography, the program remained on air for approximately 18 to 19 months, with the music programming selected by Nicchio.

Guests remembered from that period include members of Raimundos, Velhas Virgens, 365, Violeta de Outono and Gueto. At least one complete interview with Velhas Virgens survives as an MP3 recorded directly from the original broadcast and is awaiting publication.

Folha de S.Paulo — “Órfãos do Rock”

On April 23, 2001, only days after Joey Ramone's death, Marcelo Nicchio appeared in the cover story of Folhateen, the youth supplement of Folha de S.Paulo. The printed cover line was “Órfãos do Rock” (“Orphans of Rock”); the inside article, also preserved in Folha's official digital archive, was titled “Onde estão os ídolos?” (“Where are the idols?”).

The personal archive preserves scans of the cover and inside pages. The article identifies Nicchio by name and includes his comments on Joey Ramone, punk culture, the do-it-yourself principle, the Brazilian underground market and the continuity of rock music.

Dual preservation

This is one of the clearest examples in the archive: Folha's official website preserves the text and identification, while the scans preserve the printed cover, original layout and visual context.

Prateleira Cultural — Sinal Verde magazine

Between 2003 and 2006, Nicchio regularly wrote the Prateleira Cultural column for Sinal Verde magazine. A surviving set of roughly twenty unique editorial pieces shows that the column was broader than entertainment reviewing: it worked as a space for observing culture, media and technological change.

The preserved scans include writing on the Ramones, Kurt Cobain, Seu Jorge, the band 365, Brazilian cinema, television, literature and cultural venues, as well as a clear series of digital subjects: Skype and VoIP, Google Earth, RSS/XML, internet radio and Orkut.

This documentation matters to the larger biography because it shows, in contemporary material, that the relationship between culture and digital systems was not invented retrospectively to connect his media work with the later Search career.

Bylines and name identity

The Sinal Verde pages themselves document several byline variants: Marcelo Nicchio, Marcelo Henrique Nicchio and Marcelo H. Nicchio. Some pages also describe him as a “musician and cultural producer”, creating a direct documentary bridge between the music career and the editorial activity.
