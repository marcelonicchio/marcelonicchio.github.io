Full biography

Marcelo Nicchio is a Brazilian professional and creator whose trajectory spans original music, communication, digital culture, audiovisual work, Search/Performance and, more recently, independent Human–AI Interaction research. This page organizes those fields as parts of one lifetime rather than disconnected careers.

A trajectory built in layers

Marcelo Nicchio's biography does not fit comfortably under a single professional label. The chronology begins in music at the end of the 1980s and moves, with substantial overlap, through radio, print media, BBS culture, commercial internet, audiovisual creation, Search Engine Marketing, performance media and research involving Artificial Intelligence systems.

The link between these fields is not an attempt to retroactively turn them into one profession. It is the recurrence of creation, technology, mediation, circulation of information, audiences and human behavior. Across different decades, that territory took the form of a band, a radio program, a BBS, a digital portal, a Search operation, a television program or a research methodology involving generative systems.

Music, the independent scene and public presence

In 1989–1990, while still a teenager, Nicchio played snare drum in the Mocidade Alegre samba-school percussion section. Between 1990 and 1991 he studied at Conservatório Antonino Simalha and by 1991 was already playing drums in cover material.

In 1992 he founded Coitado do Próximo, an original project in which he worked as composer and drummer. The band began performing in 1993. In 1994 it recorded the demo Pegue o seu Celular / Mulher SP, took part in an interview with Kid Vinil on 97FM, and opened a performance by the band 365 at Aeroanta.

The first album, Meu Querido Diário, was recorded in 1997 at Estúdio Asas, produced by Edson X and featuring guests connected to Velhas Virgens and 365. In 1999–2000 came pre-production for Eu Não Tô Nem Aí, co-produced by Fábio “Badi” Crepaldi and Marcelo Nicchio.

In July 2000 Coitado do Próximo opened for Os Paralamas do Sucesso at the Olympia in São Paulo. On June 21, 2001, the band appeared on Programa do Jô on Rede Globo, performing Estava Comigo, a composition by Nicchio. The band ended its activities in 2003 and reunited for a one-off performance in December 2023.

Surviving archive

Records survive from the Olympia show, Programa do Jô, the first show, recording sessions, Café Piu Piu and the 2023 reunion, together with preserved audio of the Kid Vinil interview. Part of this material is still awaiting digitization or public release.

Radio, press and cultural criticism

In 1993, Nicchio maintained a cultural column on Tremonte BBS. Between 1994 and 1995 he presented and curated the weekday Metrópole program on Rádio Nova Paulista FM 103.7, from 8 p.m. to 10 p.m., for approximately 18 to 19 months.

On April 23, 2001, he participated in a cover story for Folhateen, the youth supplement of Folha de S.Paulo, prompted by the death of Joey Ramone. The printed cover line was Órfãos do Rock (“Orphans of Rock”); the internal article appears in Folha's official archive as Onde estão os ídolos? (“Where are the idols?”).

Between 2003 and 2006 he regularly wrote the Prateleira Cultural column for Sinal Verde magazine. The surviving corpus contains roughly twenty unique editorial pieces covering music, film, television, literature, radio, behavior and digital shifts such as Skype/VoIP, Google Earth, RSS/XML, Orkut and internet radio.

BBS culture, commercial internet and digital products

Between 1992 and 1996, Nicchio participated in BBS environments including SP Online/STI and Mandic. During this period he created Minduim BBS, a personal experiment built with RemoteAccess and two telephone lines. No surviving original screenshots are currently known; the project remains an autobiographical record.

In 1996 he began using commercial internet access through STI. In 2000 he worked at PSINet, the multinational hosting and infrastructure company, in corporate hosting sales.

Between 2004 and 2008 he created and operated Mirantte News, a regional services and content portal. Preserved contemporary material identifies Marcelo Nicchio as director of the site and describes its categories, commercial hot-sites and MapLink map/routing integration. Founder status is part of the autobiographical account and is kept distinct from what public sources directly verify.

Audiovisual: creation, direction and production

In 1998, Nicchio worked in executive production for Homens na Cozinha, broadcast by Band, focusing on fundraising. Between 1998 and 2000, through Nicchio & Associados, he also contributed to fundraising connected to the feature films Carandiru and Domésticas.

In 2006 he was credited as Artistic Director and co-Executive Producer of Meia-Noite e Uns, a cultural television program broadcast by RedeTV! and hosted by Soninha Francine. Surviving end credits directly confirm those roles and identify Dreamsnetwork.tv as the production entity. Artistic concept, naming history and other behind-the-scenes details remain autobiographical where no independent public record exists.

Later projects include O Arquiteto da Apoteose, a documentary begun in 2019 about Sidnei França and still unfinished, and Autópsia, a content project developed during the pandemic and presented to potential partners. Both are treated in this hub as works in development, not released productions.

Search, performance and digital systems

Between 2008 and 2009, Nicchio deepened his work in SEM and completed certifications under the Google Advertising Professional program. Search and performance media then became a long-term professional axis.

At CookieWEB, from 2010 to 2012, he progressed from coordination to SEM management. In 2012–2013 he worked at Petlove on the internalization of digital media. In 2014 he worked at BEST, within the Kenshoo ecosystem, and taught advanced Search and Social segmentation workshops for E-Commerce Brasil. In 2015 he worked at Ad.Dialetto.

In parallel, from 2012 onward, he developed Clickland Digital, an agency focused on small and medium-sized businesses, created with Carlos Portela. Preserved historical material documents the agency's existence, its public “Premium Search Agency” positioning, Nicchio's direct association and Google program badges displayed at the time. Among later roles, he worked remotely at Driven.CX in 2023–2024.

Independent Human–AI Interaction research

From 2025 onward, Nicchio began developing an independent Human–AI Interaction research cycle. The four currently catalogued Melissa 1.0 works were deposited on Zenodo in January 2026, each with its own DOI.

In May 2026, the cycle expanded with The Punk Rock Orchestra: A Single-Operator Methodology for Adversarial Epistemic Triangulation in Human–AI Interaction Research. Version 1 has instances on OSF, SSRN, Zenodo, PubPub and GitHub; this hub treats those records as instances of one work/version rather than five separate publications.

Punk Rock Orchestra v2 is currently in development and is intended as the definitive consolidation of this specific cycle. No future DOI, repository deposit or formal publication is asserted before it exists publicly. There is also currently no fixed public research agenda beyond v2.

Name variants and public identity

The canonical name used throughout this hub is Marcelo Nicchio. Historical sources and records may also use Marcelo Henrique Nicchio, Marcelo H. Nicchio and Marcelo Minduim. These forms are reconciled as variants of the same entity while source wording is preserved where necessary and unrelated namesakes are kept separate.
