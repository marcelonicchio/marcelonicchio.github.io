Full biography

Marcelo Nicchio is a Brazilian professional and creator whose trajectory spans original music, communication, digital culture, audiovisual work, Search/Performance and, more recently, independent Human–AI Interaction research. This page organizes those fields as parts of one lifetime rather than disconnected careers.

A trajectory built in layers

Marcelo Nicchio's biography does not fit comfortably under a single professional label. His public trajectory begins in music at the end of the 1980s, moves through radio, print media, BBS culture, commercial internet, audiovisual creation, Search Engine Marketing and performance media, and eventually reaches independent research in Artificial Intelligence and Human–AI Interaction.

The connection between these fields is not an attempt to retroactively turn them into one profession. It is the recurrence of the same territory: creation, systems of mediation, technology, circulation of information, audiences and human behavior. At different points, that territory took the form of a band, a radio program, an experimental BBS, a digital portal, a Search operation, a television program or a research methodology involving generative systems.

Music, the independent scene and public presence

The first major axis of Nicchio's trajectory is musical. After early experience with percussion and formal musical study, he founded the original project Coitado do Próximo in 1992, acting as composer and drummer. The band was shaped by Brazilian and international punk rock influences and began performing in São Paulo clubs in 1993.

In 1994 the group recorded its first demo tape, featuring the original songs Pegue o seu Celular and Mulher SP. During the same period Nicchio was interviewed by Kid Vinil on 97FM Rock, while Coitado do Próximo became active in the São Paulo live circuit, including shows alongside and opening for the band 365.

The first original album, Meu Querido Diário, was recorded in 1997 at Estúdio Asas in São Paulo, produced by Edson X. The project included guest appearances by musicians connected to Velhas Virgens and 365. By the end of 1999 and beginning of 2000, the band was preparing its second album, Eu Não Tô Nem Aí, co-produced by Fábio “Badi” Crepaldi and Marcelo Nicchio.

In July 2000 Coitado do Próximo performed at the Olympia in São Paulo as the opening act for Os Paralamas do Sucesso. On June 21, 2001, the band was interviewed and performed on Programa do Jô, broadcast by Rede Globo, playing Estava Comigo, a composition by Nicchio. The project was dissolved in 2003 after a period of internal strain and reunited for a one-off performance in 2023.

Surviving archive

Audiovisual records survive from the Olympia show, Programa do Jô, live performances and studio sessions, together with preserved audio of the Kid Vinil interview and additional material still being digitized.

Radio, press and cultural criticism

Communication developed in parallel with music. In 1993, Nicchio maintained a cultural column on Tremonte BBS, at a time when BBS networks were still one of the main environments for connected digital culture in Brazil. Between 1994 and 1995, he presented and curated the music programming of the weekday radio show Metrópole on Rádio Nova Paulista FM 103.7 in São Paulo.

In April 2001 he took part in a cover story for Folhateen, the youth supplement of Folha de S.Paulo, published after the death of Joey Ramone. The printed cover line was Órfãos do Rock (“Orphans of Rock”); the Folha digital archive carries the internal article under the title Onde estão os ídolos? (“Where are the idols?”). The original physical cover and inside pages have been preserved in Nicchio's personal archive.

Between 2003 and 2006, he regularly wrote the Prateleira Cultural column for Sinal Verde magazine. The surviving archive shows a broad editorial range: music, film, television, literature, radio, behavior and also digital transformation topics such as RSS, Skype, Google Earth, Orkut and internet radio. This body of work matters because it documents an intersection between culture and technology before his later professional focus on Search.

BBS culture, commercial internet and digital products

Nicchio's involvement with networked systems appears early in the chronology. Between 1992 and 1996 he was an active user of BBS services including SP Online/STI and Mandic. During this period he created Minduim BBS, a personal experiment built with RemoteAccess software and two telephone lines. No screenshots or technical documentation are known to have survived, so the project remains an autobiographical record of a transitional analog-digital period that was rarely archived.

The move from BBS culture to the commercial internet happened continuously from 1996 onward. In 2000, Nicchio worked at PSINet, the American hosting and infrastructure company that had absorbed STI, in corporate hosting sales.

Between 2004 and 2008 he led the creation and operation of Mirantte News, his first digital venture at greater scale. The site operated as a regional service directory and editorial portal with roughly twenty categories, commercial pages and map/routing integration through MapLink, then part of the UOL group, before Google Maps became dominant in Brazil. Preserved contemporary material identifies Nicchio as director of the site and describes both the product and its MapLink integration.

Audiovisual: creation, direction and production

Audiovisual work appears recurrently from the late 1990s onward. In 1998, Nicchio worked in executive production for the television program Homens na Cozinha, broadcast by Band, with a focus on fundraising. In the same broader period, through Nicchio & Associados, he contributed to fundraising processes connected to the feature films Carandiru and Domésticas.

In 2006 he was credited as Artistic Director and one of two Executive Producers of Meia-Noite e Uns, a pop-literature television program broadcast by RedeTV! and hosted by Soninha Francine. Surviving end credits directly confirm both functions and identify Dreamsnetwork.tv as the production company. According to Nicchio's own account, he also developed the artistic and visual concept and narrative structure; the theme music was created with Fábio “Badi” Crepaldi.

Later projects include the documentary O Arquiteto da Apoteose, about carnival designer Sidnei França, filmed from 2019 onward and still unfinished, and Autópsia, a television/content project developed during the pandemic with a cast of artists, intellectuals and countercultural figures. Both remain works in progress.

Search, performance and algorithmic systems

The Mirantte News experience brought Nicchio into contact with Search Engine Marketing professionals. Between 2008 and 2009 he specialized further in SEM and completed Google Advertising Professional certifications. From that point onward, Search and digital performance became a long-term professional axis.

At CookieWEB he progressed from coordination to SEM management, leading a team and multiple e-commerce accounts. In 2012 he was hired by Petlove to internalize the company's digital media operation, building team, tools and processes. In 2014 he worked at BEST, the Brazilian commercial arm of Kenshoo, dealing with large-scale campaigns and teaching Advanced Segmentation for Search and Advanced Segmentation for Social workshops for E-Commerce Brasil.

In 2015 he joined Ad.Dialetto, working on the Máquina de Vendas/Ricardo Eletro account. In parallel, from 2012 onward, he maintained Clickland Digital, an agency focused on small and medium-sized businesses, founded with Carlos Portela. Preserved Clickland material documents its positioning as a Search agency and Nicchio's direct professional association with the company.

Independent Human–AI Interaction research

From 2025 onward, Nicchio began developing an independent research agenda in Human–AI Interaction. The first cycle was organized around the Melissa 1.0 series, with four works deposited on Zenodo between late 2025 and January 2026, addressing relational emergence, persistent persona architecture and first-person testimony by a synthetic entity.

In May 2026 that cycle expanded into The Punk Rock Orchestra: A Single-Operator Methodology for Adversarial Epistemic Triangulation in Human–AI Interaction Research. The first version was distributed through OSF, SSRN, Zenodo, PubPub and GitHub and connected to the author's ORCID record.

Version 2.0 of the Punk Rock Orchestra is currently in development and is intended as the definitive consolidation of this specific research cycle — one that began with Melissa and was methodologically formalized through PRO. There is currently no fixed public research agenda beyond that work.

Name variants and public identity

The canonical name used throughout this hub is Marcelo Nicchio. Historical documents and authorship records may also use Marcelo Henrique Nicchio, Marcelo H. Nicchio and Marcelo Minduim, an artistic name used during part of the music period. These forms are treated as variants of the same entity while preserving the original wording of historical sources when necessary.
