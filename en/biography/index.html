Full biography

Marcelo Nicchio is a Brazilian professional and creator whose trajectory spans original music, communication, Internet & Performance, audiovisual work and, more recently, independent Human–AI Interaction research. This page presents these fields as parts of one lifetime rather than disconnected careers.

A trajectory built in layers

Marcelo Nicchio's biography does not fit comfortably into a single professional label. The chronology begins with music in the late 1980s and moves, with overlaps, through radio, press, BBS culture, the commercial internet, audiovisual creation, Search Engine Marketing, performance media and research with artificial-intelligence systems.

The link among these fields is not a retrospective attempt to turn them into one profession. It is the recurrence of creation, technology, mediation, information circulation, audiences and human behavior. In different decades, that territory took the form of a band, a radio program, a BBS, a portal, a performance operation, a television program or a research methodology involving generative systems.

1989–1991Formation
1989–1991Music

Origins and training

Nicchio began playing percussion while still a teenager, moving through samba-school percussion and, according to his biographical account, formal musical study at Conservatório Antonino Simalha. By 1991 he was already playing drums in cover projects. Original songwriting followed soon after, strongly influenced by both São Paulo punk rock and international punk.

Declared influences from that period include Ramones, Misfits, The Clash, Bad Brains and Sex Pistols, as well as Magazine/Verminose, Garotos Podres, Inocentes, Ira! and 365 in Brazil.

1992–2000Music, BBS culture, radio, internet and audiovisual work in parallel
1992–1994Music

Coitado do Próximo

In 1992, Marcelo Nicchio founded Coitado do Próximo, an original project in which he worked as composer and drummer. From the beginning, Nicchio and the vocalist formed the core of the project while the band moved through different lineups over the years. The group remained continuously active until its official end in 2003.

The first performance preserved on video took place in 1992 at Barão Homem de Mello, a state school in the Santana district of São Paulo. From 1993 onward, the band began playing more structured shows on the city's club circuit.

In 1994 it recorded its first demo tape with Pegue o seu Celular and Mulher SP. The final audio and footage from the recording sessions survive and await publication.

That same year Nicchio was interviewed by Kid Vinil on 97FM Rock. A direct recording of the radio broadcast has been preserved and is also expected to become part of the public archive.

1992–1996Internet & Performance

1992–1996 — BBS culture

Nicchio was an intensive user of BBS services including SP Online/STI and Mandic. This period marked his first sustained contact with digital communities, menu-driven navigation, file exchange, messaging and network culture before the commercial web became mainstream in Brazil.

In parallel, he created Minduim BBS, a personal experiment built with RemoteAccess software and two telephone lines. The project was experimental and non-commercial.

1993Communication & Culture

Early writing in digital environments

In 1993, Nicchio maintained a cultural column on Tremonte BBS, during a phase in which writing, cultural curation and digital environments were already beginning to overlap.

São Paulo circuit

1994–1995Communication & Culture

Metrópole — Rádio Nova Paulista FM

Between 1994 and 1995, Marcelo Nicchio hosted and curated the music programming of Metrópole on Rádio Nova Paulista FM 103.7 in São Paulo. According to his biography, the program aired Monday through Friday from 8 p.m. to 10 p.m. and remained on air for approximately 18 to 19 months.

Guests remembered from that period include members of Raimundos, Velhas Virgens, 365, Violeta de Outono and Gueto. At least one complete interview with Velhas Virgens survives as an MP3 recorded directly from the broadcast and is awaiting publication.

1996Internet & Performance

1996 — transition to the commercial internet

The move from BBS systems to the internet was continuous. From 1996 onward, Nicchio began using commercial internet access through STI, the same company that had previously provided his BBS access. Networked activity expanded beyond bulletin boards alongside the growth of Brazil's commercial internet.

2001–2008Public presence, communication, television and digital products
Apr. 23, 2001Communication & Culture

Folha de S.Paulo — “Órfãos do Rock”

On April 23, 2001, Marcelo Nicchio appeared in the cover story of Folhateen, the youth supplement of Folha de S.Paulo, published after Joey Ramone's death. The printed cover line was “Órfãos do Rock” (“Orphans of Rock”); the inside article preserved in Folha's official digital archive was titled “Onde estão os ídolos?” (“Where are the idols?”).

The personal archive preserves scans of the cover and inside pages. The article identifies Nicchio by name and records his comments on Joey Ramone, punk culture, the do-it-yourself principle, the Brazilian underground market and the persistence of rock.

2003–2006Communication & Culture

Prateleira Cultural — Sinal Verde magazine

Between 2003 and 2006, Nicchio regularly wrote the Prateleira Cultural column for Sinal Verde magazine. The surviving set contains roughly twenty unique editorial pieces, including work published in other sections.

The scans cover the Ramones, Kurt Cobain, Seu Jorge, the band 365, Brazilian cinema, television, literature, radio and cultural spaces, as well as digital subjects including Skype and VoIP, Google Earth, RSS/XML, internet radio and Orkut.

This corpus is one of the clearest documentary bridges between cultural criticism and digital culture: contemporary material shows technology already functioning as an editorial subject before the later professional specialization in Search.

2004–2008Internet & Performance

2004–2008 — Mirantte News: digital product and acquisition

Mirantte News was created and operated by Marcelo Nicchio, with Luiz Kechichian as investor-partner. The portal brought together roughly twenty service categories, combining editorial content, commercial pages and location tools.

A contemporary article identifies Marcelo Nicchio as site director and describes its commercial hot-sites, categories and MapLink map/routing integration. The practical need to generate traffic for the portal brought Nicchio into contact with Search professionals and connected digital-product work to acquisition and performance.

2006Audiovisual

2006 — Meia-Noite e Uns

Meia-Noite e Uns was a pop-literature television program broadcast by RedeTV! and hosted by Soninha Francine. Marcelo Nicchio served as Artistic Director and Executive Producer alongside Cristiano Gonçalves, through Dreamsnetwork.tv.

Nicchio created the artistic and visual concept and the narrative structure. Paulão de Carvalho took part in the writing and co-presented the program at Nicchio's invitation; the theme music was written and produced with Fábio “Badi” Crepaldi.

The intended title was originally Meia-Noite e Um (“12:01 a.m.”), linked to a blog of the same name and the idea of starting one minute after midnight. Because the exact minute could not be guaranteed in the schedule, the title became Meia-Noite e Uns.

2008–2015Search, performance and operational scale
2008–2009Internet & Performance

2008–2009 — specialization in Search Engine Marketing

From the acquisition problem faced at Mirantte News, Nicchio deepened his work in Search Engine Marketing. Between 2008 and 2009 he completed certifications under the Google Advertising Professional program. Digital systems also became a quantitative operating field involving keywords, auctions, segmentation, attribution, ROI and scale.

2010–2012Internet & Performance

2010–2012 — CookieWEB: Search at scale

At CookieWEB, Nicchio progressed from coordination to SEM Manager. He led a team of six analysts and simultaneously managed roughly 22 small and mid-sized e-commerce accounts, including Beleza na Web.

Team photographs and other professional materials remain in the personal archive and will be incorporated progressively.

2012–2013Internet & Performance

2012–2013 — Petlove: media internalization

At Petlove, Nicchio was hired to internalize the digital media operation. He built the structure, team and processes and expanded operational scale from roughly 150,000 to 3 million keywords across three accounts.

The role centered on building an internal media operation and expanding performance scale.

Sep. 2012–presentInternet & Performance

2012–present — Clickland Digital

In September 2012, Nicchio founded Clickland Digital Ltda. with Carlos Portela, focused on Search and media for small and medium-sized businesses. The agency ran alongside other professional roles and presented itself as a “Premium Search Agency”, emphasizing Search, ROI, metrics, multichannel work and Web Analytics.

Regular commercial activity later decreased, while the company remains active as an operating and backup structure. Clickland is part of the continuity of the trajectory without representing Nicchio's primary professional activity today.

2016–2020Internet & Performance

2016–2020 — independent operations

Between 2016 and 2020, Nicchio worked on digital-media and performance projects across different companies and sectors. This period maintained operational continuity outside a single agency structure.

2019Audiovisual

2019 — O Arquiteto da Apoteose

O Arquiteto da Apoteose is a documentary about carnival designer Sidnei França. Material was filmed, but the project was not finalized and remains a work in progress. Nicchio wrote and directed the documentary.

Unpublished making-of footage survives. A future public release can document the production process without presenting the project as a completed or released film.

2021Audiovisual

2021 — Autópsia

Autópsia was conceived and produced during the pandemic as an audiovisual documentary/entertainment project for streaming/pay-TV. Set at Espaço Cultural Cemitério de Automóveis in São Paulo, the format was designed as 10 one-hour episodes, combining debates with poetry, musical presentations and theatrical performances. The project reached filming and has recorded material, but it was not publicly released.

In the original presentation credits, Marcelo Nicchio is credited with the Original Idea and Artistic Direction and is part of Creation alongside Paulão de Carvalho, Julio Piconi and Eduardo Fabro. Julio Piconi is credited with General Direction, Paulão de Carvalho with Content Direction, Sidnei França with Art Direction and Eduardo Fabro with Executive Direction.

The presentation predates filming and records the concept, references, episode structure and proposed cast and guests at that stage of the project — some were realized and others were not. The filmed material remains in the archive and is not published; the original deck preserves the project's identity and personality.

2025–2026Independent AI/HAI research
2025–2026AI/HAI

A research cycle

The 2025–2026 body of work forms a specific arc. The Melissa 1.0 series documents and formalizes relational observations and persistent persona architectures. Punk Rock Orchestra v1 reorganizes part of that learning into a single-operator adversarial epistemic triangulation methodology. PRO v2, currently in development, is conceived by Nicchio as the definitive version and closure of that cycle.

Music

Authorship, credits and name variants

According to Nicchio, a significant part of the repertoire has work entries in the ABRAMUS/ECAD ecosystem. Access to the ABRAMUS account is currently being restored, so the hub does not yet use that catalogue as a fully consolidated public source on a work-by-work basis.

Repertoire listings and authorship credits will not be described as formal copyright registration in the legal sense. Until the ABRAMUS catalogue is checked, public music-platform credits and surviving liner material may serve as complementary sources.

For canonical identity, this hub prioritizes Marcelo Nicchio. Historical records may also appear under Marcelo Henrique Nicchio, Marcelo H. Nicchio or Marcelo Minduim. When a source uses a historical variant, the original form will be preserved and linked back to the same entity.

Compositions already represented in the biographical and audiovisual archive include Estava Comigo, Ciúmes, Pegue o seu Celular and Mulher SP.
